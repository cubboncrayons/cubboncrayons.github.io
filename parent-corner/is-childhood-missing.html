Is Your Child's Calendar Full, but Their Childhood Missing?

School. Homework. Classes. Where is the time to simply play?

By Cubbon Crayons · Published 22 November 2026

At 6:30 on a Wednesday evening, six-year-old Appi comes home from school. Her brother is playing with blocks. “Amma, can I play with him?” Her mother checks the clock. “First homework, then drawing class.” “But Amma, just five minutes!”

Her mother wants every opportunity for her daughter. But she wonders: Are we preparing children for tomorrow while leaving too little room for them to enjoy today?

When every hour becomes a lesson

Swimming, music and sports can enrich childhood. Children also need time without adult instructions: making up games, experimenting, negotiating turns and exploring. The American Academy of Pediatrics highlights play's role in learning, relationships and emotional development. [1]

Play is not only a reward after learning. Play is one of the ways children learn.

A Kannada educational perspective

Kannada writer and educationist Dr. Shivaram Karanth placed importance on children's interests, activity and experience. His educational thinking and work at Balavana provide a valuable lens for looking at unstructured play. This is an interpretation drawn from literary commentary, not a verbatim quotation from his original books. [2]

A cardboard box can be a rocket, a bus or a shop. The child who imagines a journey is not necessarily wasting time; they are experimenting with possibilities.

A Bengaluru perspective

Former cricketer Rahul Dravid spoke at a Bengaluru 2040 event about creating conditions in which children can play more easily. City traffic, development and a lack of accessible space complicate that goal. His remarks are paraphrased from reporting, not quoted verbatim. [3]

Some families have overcrowded schedules; others struggle with time, safe spaces and commuting. Neither challenge should become an excuse to blame parents.

Four practical ways to make room

  1. Protect a little unscheduled time

    Not every evening needs another planned activity.

  2. Let children lead sometimes

    When a child calls the sofa a mountain, ask how to climb it safely instead of changing the game.

  3. Notice enjoyment, not only achievement

    After class, ask “What did you enjoy today?” as well as “What did you learn?”

  4. Use the space you have

    Safe outdoor play is wonderful when accessible. Indoor pretend play, drawing and blocks matter too.

Tonight's activity: The Magic Box

Take a clean cardboard box and ask: “What could this become?” Let your child decide: bus, rocket, shop or treasure chest.

Follow your child's lead

“Where is our rocket going?”

“What are you selling in your shop?”

No marks and no single correct answer; just a few minutes of shared imagination.

A thought to carry home

Childhood isn't merely preparation for adulthood. It has its own curiosity, friendships, laughter and memories.

Let us prepare our children for tomorrow without taking away their opportunity to enjoy today.
