Sportsmanship · Ages 2–10 · Article 07

What should you do when your child cries after losing a game?

Help children handle disappointment without taking away the joy of play.

By Cubbon Crayons · Published 27 November 2026

Appi, Amma and Ajja were playing Chaukabara. When Ajja won, tears filled Appi’s eyes. “I don’t want to play anymore!”

Make room for disappointment

“You really wanted to win, didn't you? Losing can feel disappointing,” Amma said. She gave him time to settle. Ajja gently invited him to play another round.

What can parents do?

Do not demand that children stop crying or change the rules every time to make them win. Set a calm boundary against throwing game pieces. Model sportsmanship by congratulating the winner when you lose.

“I can see you're disappointed. Would you like a little break?”

“We can play again, but we must not throw the pieces.”

Read • Feel • Do • Talk

Read Appi’s story and explore his feelings. Ages 2–4: take turns rolling a ball. Ages 5–7: play a simple round of Chaukabara. Ages 8–10: practise congratulating the winner and discussing fair play.

Ask: “What might help you next time you lose?”

It is okay to cry after losing. Help children find the courage to play again.

Editorial note

These are suggested family activities, not clinical recommendations. Karnataka-specific evidence about losing board games and literary quotation verification remain incomplete.
