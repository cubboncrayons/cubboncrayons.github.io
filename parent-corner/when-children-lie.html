When Your Child Tells a Lie, How Should You Respond?

Help children find the courage to tell the truth.

By Cubbon Crayons · Published 25 November 2026

Amma noticed colourful crayon marks on the wall. “Appi, did you draw on the wall?” “No, Amma!” Appi was holding a blue crayon and looked worried.

Appi’s little lie

“Were you afraid I would scold you?” Amma asked. “Yes, Amma. I drew it.” “I'm glad you told me the truth. But we shouldn't draw on walls. Come, let's clean it together.” Appi helped her clean the wall.

Why do children lie?

Children may hide mistakes because they fear punishment. Very young children may also confuse imagination and reality. Address the behaviour without labelling the child.

A lesson from Mahatma Gandhi

Gandhi describes confessing a childhood theft to his father in a written note. His father's compassionate response left a lasting impression. This is a paraphrase of the stealing and atonement episode in his autobiography, not a direct quotation.

Words you could try

“Tell me what really happened. I'm listening.”

“How can we make this right?”

“Telling the truth is important.”

Read • Feel • Do • Talk

Read Appi's story, discuss his fear, role-play a mistake and repair it together. Ages 2–3: demonstrate cleaning spilled water with a toy. Ages 4–5: role-play the crayon incident. Ages 6–7: discuss accidents versus hiding mistakes. Ages 8–10: discuss honesty and rebuilding trust.

When our children make mistakes, may they have the courage to come to us rather than the fear that makes them hide.

Sources and limitations

Mangaluru primary-school behavioural study

This study is not representative of all Karnataka children. Age-by-age lying frequencies have not been independently verified.
