Is comparing our child with other children really helpful?

Encourage progress, not comparison.

By Cubbon Crayons · Published 24 November 2026

In this article

READ · A familiar moment

One evening, Appi was drawing. Amma noticed the neighbour’s child’s picture and said, “Look how nicely he draws. Why don't you draw like him?” Appi slowly put down his pencils. Amma meant to encourage him, but he might have wondered whether his own drawing was good enough.

FEEL · Notice the feeling

Appreciating another child's talent need not come at the expense of recognising our own child's effort. Ask how your child feels rather than assuming. Basavanna's vachana reminds us not to glorify ourselves or belittle others. This is a parenting interpretation, not a claim that the vachana was written about children.

Four things to try

Notice effort instead of ranking children. Encourage another attempt when something goes wrong. Recognise progress relative to the child's previous abilities. Appreciate the process as well as the result.

DO · Draw together

Look at an older drawing and invite your child to make a new one. For ages 2–4, enjoy choosing colours; for ages 5–7, notice something new in the drawing; for ages 8–10, let your child choose a skill to practise. Enjoy the time together rather than judging the result.

TALK · Words you could try

“I like how you're trying. Shall we try once more?”

A thought to carry home

Let another child's achievements never become the measure of your child's childhood.
